# James, son of Alphaeus

James son of Alphaeus is one of the Twelve. The New Testament lists his name four times and says nothing else certain about him. He is often called "James the Less" (Mark 15:40), but the Gospels never actually say that "James the less" and "James son of Alphaeus" are the same man. In the Western church, following Jerome (c. 383), he is also treated as **James "the brother of the Lord"**, the leader of the Jerusalem church, who was stoned in AD 62. That is the James whose death Vreugdenhil describes. The Eastern churches count them as two different men. They tell of James son of Alphaeus preaching in southern Palestine and being crucified in Egypt, or stoned in Jerusalem. All of these mission and death stories are late (Grade D/E). For a teacher the honest answer is: we know he was an apostle and was present in Jerusalem after the Ascension. We do not know where he went or how he died.

## Competing traditions

### Identification A: James son of Alphaeus = James "the less" = James the Lord's brother (Western / Jerome)
- **Claim:** these three are one man. Under this view, "brothers" of Jesus means cousins: sons of Mary the wife of Clopas/Alphaeus, the sister of Jesus' mother.
- **Earliest clear source:** Jerome, *Against Helvidius* (*De perpetua virginitate B. Mariae*), c. 383, §§15–16: "Mary the wife of Clopas ... is the same as the wife of Alphæus"; James the less "is called in Scripture the son of Mary, not however of Mary the mother of our Lord." Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 2 (c. 393): James the Lord's brother is "the son of Joseph by another wife, as some think, but, as appears to me, the son of Mary sister of the mother of our Lord." Grade **C** (a 4th-c. writer arguing from Scripture, not from older tradition).
- **Possible earlier support:** Clement of Alexandria (*Hypotyposes* 7, c. 200, via Eusebius *HE* 2.1.4) says "there were two Jameses: one called the Just, who was thrown from the pinnacle of the temple ... and another who was beheaded." Taken literally, this leaves no room for a third James among the apostles, so it is sometimes read as merging the son of Alphaeus with the Just. This is an **inference**, not a statement by Clement. Grade B for the quotation, but the inference is disputed.
- **Biblical argument used:** Gal 1:19 seems to count the Lord's brother among the apostles. Mark 15:40 ("Mary the mother of James the less and of Joses") with Mark 6:3 (Jesus' brothers "James, and Joses"). John 19:25 ("Mary the wife of Clopas").
- **Modern defenders:** Catholic Encyclopedia (Camerlynck, 1910): "by far the most probable."
- **Notes:** The Roman feast "Philip and James" (1 May until 1955, then 11 May, since 1969 on 3 May) treats this James as James the Less = the Lord's brother. It comes from the dedication of the Basilica of the Twelve Apostles in Rome (6th c.).

### Identification B: three (or more) different men (Eastern; most Protestant and modern critical scholars)
- **Claim:** James son of Alphaeus (apostle), James the Lord's brother (a son of Joseph or of Mary, not one of the Twelve), and "James the less" (Mark 15:40, uncertain) are separate people.
- **Basis:** John 7:5 ("neither did his brethren believe in him") reads awkwardly if one of the brothers was already among the Twelve. Acts 1:13–14 lists the apostles and then *separately* "his brethren." Grade A for the texts; the conclusion is interpretation.
- **Liturgical expression:** the Byzantine calendar keeps **9 October** for James son of Alphaeus and **23 October** for James the Brother of the Lord. The Coptic/Ethiopic calendars keep **10 Amshir / Yäkatit (≈ 4 Feb)** for the martyrdom of the son of Alphaeus (NASSCAL e-Clavis).
- **Note:** we found no single citable "majority view" survey. Most modern NT commentators distinguish the Lord's brother from the son of Alphaeus. UNVERIFIED as a quantified majority.

### Side issue: Levi son of Alphaeus (Mark 2:14)
Mark 2:14 calls the tax collector Levi "the son of Alphaeus". Matt 9:9 calls him Matthew. Some ancient and modern readers suggest James and Matthew/Levi were brothers. The NT never says so, and the father may simply be a different Alphaeus (a common name). Byzantine tradition makes James son of Alphaeus a tax collector too (Sanidopoulos 2017). Grade E for the kinship.

### Side issue: "Mary of Clopas" and Clopas = Alphaeus
John 19:25 names "Mary the wife of Clopas". The claim that Clopas and Alphaeus are the same name (from Aramaic *Ḥalphai*) goes back to Jerome's reading and later philology. It is not an ancient tradition. Hegesippus (via Eusebius *HE* 3.11) says Clopas was Joseph's **brother**, which is a different family tree. Grade C, contested.

### Mission traditions (no early source)
- **Southern Palestine (Eleutheropolis, Gaza):** Byzantine synaxaria. Grade E.
- **Egypt / Ostrakine:** Synaxarion of Constantinople. Grade D/E.
- **Edessa (with Andrew):** reported by Sanidopoulos without a named primary source. **UNVERIFIED**, grade E.
- **Persia / Syria:** repeated in popular websites (e.g. thecollector.com, kjvstudy.org). We found **no named ancient or medieval source**. **UNVERIFIED**, grade E. Do not use without a primary source.

## Death / martyrdom

### 1. Stoned in Jerusalem, AD 62: belongs to *James the Lord's brother* ("the Just")
- **Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.9.1 (§200), c. 93–94:** with "Festus ... now dead, and Albinus ... but upon the road," the high priest Ananus "assembled the sanhedrim of judges, and brought before them the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James, and some others ... he delivered them to be stoned." This dates the death to **AD 62**: Festus died in office in 62 and Albinus was procurator from 62 to 64. Grade: early independent source. The README puts NT/archaeology at A and early Christian writers at B, so we use **B** for this Jewish historian writing within 60 years.
- **Hegesippus (c. 170–180), quoted in Eusebius *HE* 2.23.4–18:** James is set on the pinnacle of the temple and thrown down, then stoned. He prays "I entreat you, Lord God our Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do." A priest "of the sons of Rechab" cries "Stop ... The just one prays for you," and "one of them, who was a fuller, took the club with which he beat out clothes and struck the just man on the head." Grade **B** (Hegesippus) / **C** (relayed by Eusebius). Hegesippus links it immediately to Vespasian's siege (2.23.18), which conflicts with Josephus' date of 62.
- **Clement of Alexandria** (via *HE* 2.1.4): thrown from the pinnacle and "beaten to death with a club by a fuller." Grade B.
- **Vreugdenhil ch. 1 retells exactly Hegesippus' version.** "The Jews killed the apostle James in Jerusalem. They took him to the roof of the temple and shoved him over the edge. He landed on the ground with a dull thud. Some writers claim that James still lived after that fall ... picked up stones and flung them at the apostle James ... 'Lord, God, I pray you to forgive them, for they do not know what they do.' ... A priest, one of those who had stoned him, hears ... 'Be quiet; this righteous man is praying for us.' However, one of the Jews, in devilish rage, swings a massive club onto the head of the apostle James. The faithful servant of the Lord falls dead." Vreugdenhil calls this James "the apostle", which fits the Western Identification A. He does not say "son of Alphaeus" and does not name the Lord's brother. **Teachers should know:** this is not James son of Zebedee (Acts 12:2), and it is the son of Alphaeus only if one accepts Jerome's identification.

### 2. Stoned in Jerusalem under Claudius (41–54) and buried by the temple
- *Martyrdom of James son of Alphaeus* (Coptic Sahidic, Arabic, Ethiopic; ECCA 120). James preaches in Jerusalem, is accused before the emperor Claudius, is stoned, and is buried beside the temple. Grade **D**. This story seems to be a doublet of the Hegesippus account.
- Ps.-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve Apostles* (Byzantine-era compilation, falsely attributed): "James the son of Alphaeus, when preaching in Jerusalem, was stoned to death by the Jews, and was buried there beside the temple." Grade **D**.

### 3. Crucified at Ostrakine, Egypt
- Synaxarion of the Church of Constantinople (10th c.), 9 October: crucified by the Egyptians/pagans at Ostrakine. Followed by the *Prologue from Ochrid* (Velimirović): "He suffered in Egypt in the town of Ostracina, being crucified by the pagans." Grade **D/E**. The exact column in Delehaye's edition is UNVERIFIED.
- The Menologion of Basil II (c. 1000) *illustrates* him being stoned, while the Synaxarion text says crucified. The Byzantine tradition itself is inconsistent (Sanidopoulos 2017).

### 4. Sawn asunder / other
Mentioned in popular compilations without a source. **UNVERIFIED**, grade E.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **Identity is the main problem.** Every death story depends on whether James son of Alphaeus is the Lord's brother (West/Jerome) or a different man (East). The map should probably show the AD 62 Jerusalem death labelled "if = James the Lord's brother", or put it in a separate James-the-Just file. Coordinate with whoever covers James the Just / James son of Zebedee.
2. **Vreugdenhil's "apostle James" (ch. 1)** is the Hegesippus James the Just. A teacher may assume he means James son of Zebedee (Acts 12:2, killed by Herod with the sword), which would be wrong.
3. **Date conflict for James the Just:** Josephus puts it in AD 62; Hegesippus implies just before Vespasian's siege (c. 67–69). Most scholars follow Josephus.
4. **Synaxarion of Constantinople, 9 Oct (Ostrakine):** the primary text was not consulted. The Delehaye column needs checking.
5. **Persia/Syria and Edessa traditions:** no primary source found. Treat as UNVERIFIED until one is found.
6. **Budge's *Contendings*:** page reference for the Martyrdom not verified.
7. The "majority scholarly view" on distinguishing the son of Alphaeus from the Lord's brother is asserted from general knowledge, with no survey cited. UNVERIFIED.
