# Peter (Simon Peter, Cephas)

Simon, a fisherman from Bethsaida who lived in Capernaum, was brought to Jesus by his brother Andrew and renamed Cephas/Peter ("rock"). After the Ascension, Peter was the main spokesman of the church in Jerusalem. At Pentecost he preached to the crowd. He then went out to Samaria, Lydda, Joppa and Caesarea, where the Roman officer Cornelius was the first Gentile convert. Herod Agrippa I imprisoned him, and he escaped. After the Jerusalem meeting about the Gentiles and a clash with Paul at Antioch, the New Testament no longer tells where he went. Writers from the end of the first century onward agree that he died as a martyr in Rome under Nero (c. 64–68). From about AD 200 they say he was crucified, and from Origen and the *Acts of Peter* onward that he was crucified head-downward. The New Testament does not describe his death or say where it happened.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition 1: Peter as founder and first bishop of the church at Antioch
- **Claim:** Peter led the Antioch church before Evodius and Ignatius.
- **Earliest source:** Origen (c. 230s–240s), *Homily 6 on Luke*, says Ignatius was the *second* bishop of Antioch after Peter (reported in modern reference works; the Origen text itself is UNVERIFIED in this session). Eusebius, *HE* 3.36.2 (c. 300–325) says plainly: Ignatius "chosen bishop of Antioch, second in succession to Peter". *HE* 3.22 makes Evodius the first and Ignatius the second.
- **Grade:** C.
- **NT basis:** Gal 2:11 shows Peter in Antioch, but not leading the church there. Acts 13:1 lists the Antioch leaders without Peter.
- **Notes:** Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 1 has Peter in Rome only "after having been bishop of the church of Antioch".

### Tradition 2: Peter visited Corinth
- **Claim:** Peter and Paul jointly planted the church at Corinth.
- **Earliest source:** Dionysius, bishop of Corinth, *Letter to the Romans* (c. 170), quoted in Eusebius, *HE* 2.25.8. Grade B.
- **NT:** 1 Cor 1:12 and 3:22 show a "Cephas" group at Corinth (A). 1 Cor 9:5 shows Peter travelling with his wife (A). Neither says Peter was there.
- **Notes:** Paul calls himself the planter at Corinth (1 Cor 3:6; 4:15), which is in tension with Dionysius. Scholars disagree on whether a Petrine visit is likely.

### Tradition 3: Peter preached in the regions of 1 Peter 1:1 (Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, Bithynia)
- **Earliest source:** Origen, *Commentary on Genesis* III, quoted in Eusebius, *HE* 3.1.2: Peter "appears to have preached". Grade C. Eusebius himself (*HE* 3.4.2) derives it from the letter's address.
- **Later:** Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 1; Pseudo-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* 1 (date uncertain, probably late/Byzantine; grade D) adds "Betania" (Bithynia) and Italy.
- **Notes:** The letter's address shows only that the author wrote to Christians there (A). It does not show that Peter travelled there.

### Tradition 4: "Babylon" in 1 Pet 5:13 is a code-name for Rome
- **Earliest source:** Eusebius, *HE* 2.15.2, citing Clement of Alexandria, *Hypotyposes* VIII, and Papias: "they say that he wrote [1 Peter] in Rome itself, as is indicated by him, when he calls the city, by a figure, Babylon". It is not fully clear whether the Babylon=Rome note is Clement's/Papias's or Eusebius's own. Grade B if from Papias or Clement, C if Eusebius's own inference.
- **Supporting usage:** Revelation uses "Babylon" for Rome (Rev 14:8; 17:5, 9, 18; 18:2) (A for the usage).
- **Majority view:** Most modern commentators accept Babylon = Rome. *Citation for the "majority" claim UNVERIFIED in this session.* See e.g. Bockmuehl 2012.

### Tradition 5: "Babylon" is literal Babylon in Mesopotamia
- **Source:** John Calvin, *Commentary on 1 Peter* 5:13 (1551): "it is very probable that he was at Babylon" (CCEL). Calvin rejects the Rome reading as an "old comment" with "no colour of truth". Erasmus held a similar view (not checked).
- **Grade:** not graded (Reformation-era reading). No ancient source places Peter in Mesopotamia.
- **Place:** Babylon, Iraq ([Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/893951)).

### Tradition 6: "Babylon" is the Roman fortress of Babylon in Egypt (Old Cairo)
- **Source:** Coptic tradition linking Mark and the Babylon fortress. Earliest written source UNVERIFIED in this session. Grade E.
- **Place:** Babylon/al-Fustat/Old Cairo ([Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/727082)).

### Tradition 7: Peter came to Rome early (under Claudius) and was its bishop for 25 years
- **Sources:** Eusebius, *HE* 2.14.6 (c. 300–325): Peter came "during the reign of Claudius … to Rome against this great corrupter", i.e. Simon Magus. Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 1 (392): "second year of Claudius … held the sacerdotal chair there for twenty-five years until … the fourteenth year of Nero". Grade C.
- **Background:** Justin Martyr, *1 Apol.* 26 (c. 150, quoted in *HE* 2.13.3) places Simon Magus in Rome under Claudius but does **not** mention Peter (B for Simon only). The Peter–Simon Magus contest in Rome comes from the *Acts of Peter* (D).
- **Against:** Paul's letter to the Romans (c. 57) greets many people in Rome but not Peter (Rom 16). Acts 28 (Paul in Rome, c. 60–62) does not mention Peter. Acts 12:17–15:7 puts Peter in Jerusalem c. 48/49, and Gal 2:11 in Antioch. Most modern scholars reject the 25-year episcopate. *Citation for the majority position UNVERIFIED; see Cullmann 1953, Bockmuehl 2012.*

### Tradition 8: Peter and Paul jointly founded the Roman church
- **Source:** Irenaeus, *Adv. Haer.* 3.3.2 (c. 180): "the Church founded and organized at Rome by the two most glorious apostles, Peter and Paul". Grade B.
- **Against:** A Roman church existed before either apostle arrived (Rom 1:8–13; 15:22–23) (A).

### Tradition 9: Peter was never in Rome (modern minority view)
- **Source:** Otto Zwierlein, *Petrus in Rom: Die literarischen Zeugnisse* (Berlin/New York: de Gruyter, 2009; 2nd ed. 2010). He argues the Rome tradition is a 2nd-century literary construct. Reviewed in BMCR 2010.03.25.
- **Response:** Most specialists still accept a Roman martyrdom, e.g. Bockmuehl 2012. Not graded (modern scholarship).

### Tradition 10: "Quo vadis?" (Peter flees Rome and turns back)
- **Source:** *Acts of Peter* 35 (Vercelli Latin, c. 180–190). Grade D.
- **Vreugdenhil:** not told.

## Death / martyrdom

**Year range:** c. 64–68. The window is Nero's persecution after the fire of Rome (July 64; Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44) up to Nero's death (June 68). Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 1 gives "the fourteenth year of Nero" (= 67/68). Eusebius's *Chronicle* also has a late-60s date (UNVERIFIED in this session). Scholars disagree between 64/65 and 67/68. The feast of 29 June does **not** record the death date: the Roman *Depositio Martyrum* (Chronograph of 354) lists "Petri in Catacumbas et Pauli Ostense, Tusco et Basso cons." (= AD 258), a commemoration or possible translation date.

### Account A: Martyrdom (manner unspecified), implied Rome
- 1 Clement 5.4 (Rome, c. 96): "Peter, through unrighteous envy, endured not one or two, but numerous labours; and when he had at length suffered martyrdom, departed to the place of glory." Grade B.
- Ignatius, *Rom.* 4.3 (c. 110): "I do not, as Peter and Paul, issue commandments unto you". This implies a special link of both apostles with the Roman church. Grade B.
- *Ascension of Isaiah* 4:2–3 (Nero as Beliar; "one of the twelve will be given over into his hands"). Probably late 1st or early 2nd century; widely read as Peter. Grade B/D: it is an apocryphal apocalypse, but early. Its date is disputed: one view dates it before 68, another c. 100–130.
- Dionysius of Corinth (c. 170) in *HE* 2.25.8: Peter and Paul "taught together in like manner in Italy, and suffered martyrdom at the same time". Grade B.

### Account B: Crucifixion
- John 21:18–19: "you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you (gird you) and carry you where you do not want to go … to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God." Grade A, but only as a prophecy; crucifixion is an interpretation.
- Tertullian, *Scorpiace* 15 (c. 203–213): "At Rome Nero was the first who stained with blood the rising faith. Then is Peter girt by another, when he is made fast to the cross." Also *Praescr.* 36 (c. 200): at Rome "Peter endures a passion like his Lord's". Grade B.
- Lactantius, *De mort. pers.* 2 (c. 315): Nero "crucified Peter, and slew Paul". Grade C.

### Account C: Crucified head-downward
- *Acts of Peter* 37–38 (c. 180–190): the earliest narrative; Peter asks to be crucified head-down. Grade D.
- Origen, *Comm. Gen.* III (c. 230s), in *HE* 3.1.2: "having come to Rome, he was crucified head-downwards; for he had requested that he might suffer in this way." Grade C. Origen may depend on the *Acts of Peter*.
- Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 1: "nailed to the cross with his head towards the ground … asserting that he was unworthy to be crucified in the same manner as his Lord". Grade C.
- **Vreugdenhil** (ch. 1 "probably upside down"; ch. 6 "crucified upside down") follows this line.

### Burial
- Gaius (Rome, c. 200), in *HE* 2.25.7: "if you will go to the Vatican or to the Ostian way, you will find the trophies of those who laid the foundations of this church." Grade B.
- Archaeology: Vatican necropolis excavations (1940–49). A small aedicula (the "Trophy of Gaius") stands against the "Red Wall". Tile stamps of c. 146–161 date the wall and drain to just after the middle of the 2nd century. "Wall G" graffiti date from the late 3rd to early 4th century. **This confirms a 2nd-century memorial on the Vatican; it does not prove that Peter's body lay there.** Margherita Guarducci's identification of bones as Peter's (announced by Paul VI, 1968) is disputed. Grade B (memorial); disputed/E (bones).
- Competing site: the *memoria* "ad Catacumbas" on the Via Appia (San Sebastiano), with 3rd-century graffiti to Peter and Paul, and the 258 entry in the *Depositio Martyrum*. This may be a temporary translation or a cult site. Grade C (354 text); interpretation disputed.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **When did Peter reach Rome?** The NT is silent. Rom 16 (c. 57) and Acts 28 (c. 60–62) do not mention him. Jerome's arrival in 42 and 25-year episcopate are late (C) and widely rejected. For the map, an arrival "c. 60–64?" is the most defensible, but no ancient source gives it.
2. **Where did Peter go after Acts 12:17 ("another place")?** Only Antioch (Gal 2:11) and Jerusalem (Acts 15) are certain afterwards.
3. **Corinth and the 1 Peter regions:** these rest on Dionysius (B) and Origen (C). Neither is NT evidence of a visit.
4. **Authorship and date of 1 and 2 Peter** are disputed. Their geography (1 Pet 1:1; 5:13) is only Petrine if the letters are.
5. **Year of death:** 64/65 (Tacitus's persecution) or 67/68 (Jerome). Scholars disagree.
6. **The head-downward crucifixion** first appears in apocryphal *Acts of Peter* (D) and then in Origen (C). Vreugdenhil relies on this tradition, though he says "probably".
7. **Bones under St Peter's:** the 2nd-century memorial is secure; the identification of human remains is not.
8. The *Apocalypse of Peter* allusion is left out of the table for that reason.
