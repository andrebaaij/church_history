# Paul (Saul of Tarsus)

Saul was a Jew from Tarsus who was trained in the Law in Jerusalem. At first he persecuted the followers of Jesus. On the road to Damascus the risen Lord met him, and he became "the apostle to the Gentiles". Over about twenty-five years he travelled thousands of kilometres by road and sea through Syria, Asia Minor (modern Turkey) and Greece, founding churches and writing letters to them, thirteen of which are in our New Testament. He was arrested in Jerusalem, held for two years in Caesarea, survived a shipwreck on Malta, and arrived in Rome as a prisoner. Acts ends there. The earliest Christian writers add that he was finally beheaded in Rome under the emperor Nero. Some of them also say he travelled as far west as Spain first.

> **How to read this file.** Grades follow `research/README.md`. Acts and Paul's letters are grade A. Dates are *not* stated in the New Testament, though. Every year below is a modern reconstruction, built from a few fixed points (see **Chronological anchors**). The middle column of each date range is the common "Gallio-anchored" chronology. Scholars who start from Paul's letters alone (e.g. Knox, Lüdemann) arrive at quite different, earlier dates for the middle period. Bible references are to standard chapter:verse numbering. **Relative chronology from Paul himself (Gal 1:15–2:1).** After his conversion Paul went to Arabia and returned to Damascus. "Then after three years" he went up to Jerusalem (Gal 1:18). "Then after fourteen years" he went up again, with Barnabas and Titus (Gal 2:1). It is disputed whether the 14 years count from the conversion or from the first visit, and whether ancient inclusive counting applies. That leaves a range of about 13–17 years from conversion to the second visit. Most scholars identify the second visit (Gal 2:1–10) with the Jerusalem Council of Acts 15 (c. 48/49), which puts the conversion at **c. 31–36** (most often 33–34).

## Paul's letters as evidence

Paul's letters are the only first-hand writings by any apostle that are certainly from his own hand. They often fix the order of events better than Acts does. Authorship categories below reflect the common critical consensus. Many conservative scholars, and the confessional tradition behind Vreugdenhil, hold all thirteen to be genuine. Jerome (*Vir. ill.* 5) lists the same thirteen and says Hebrews "is not considered his". Hebrews is not treated here as Pauline evidence.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition A — Paul released after Acts 28, travelled again, second imprisonment
- **Claim:** after the two years of Acts 28:30, Paul was acquitted or released. He travelled again (east and/or west), was re-arrested, and died in a second Roman imprisonment.
- **Earliest source:** implied by **1 Clement 5.7** (c. 96; B), *if* "the extreme limit of the west" means Spain. Explicitly by the **Muratorian Fragment** lines 38–39 (B if dated c. 170–200). As a narrative framework: **Eusebius, *HE* 2.22.2** (c. 300–325, C): "it is said that the apostle was sent again upon the ministry of preaching, and that upon coming to the same city a second time he suffered martyrdom. In this imprisonment he wrote his second epistle to Timothy."
- **NT support (A, but indirect):** Paul's expectation of release (Phil 1:25; 2:24; Philem 22); the Pastorals' itinerary (row 50) does not fit within Acts; 2 Tim 4:16–17's "first defence".
- **Later sources:** Jerome, *Vir. ill.* 5 (C), repeats Eusebius.
- **Grade:** release itself **C** as an explicit statement. B as an inference from 1 Clement + Muratorian.
- **Vreugdenhil:** implicitly yes (ch. 1: Spain, then "imprisoned and beheaded in Rome").

### Tradition B — No release: Paul died at the end of (or soon after) the Acts 28 imprisonment, c. 62–64/65
- **Claim:** the "two whole years" ended with Paul's execution, perhaps c. 62, or in the Neronian persecution of 64–65. The Pastorals are later works, so their itinerary is not evidence.
- **Arguments:** Acts 20:25, 38 ("you will see my face no more"); Luke's silence; doubts about the Pastorals.
- **Earliest source:** none ancient states this. It is a modern scholarly reconstruction, commonly held among critical scholars (e.g. J. Murphy-O'Connor, *Paul: A Critical Life*, 1996 — page UNVERIFIED).
- **Grade:** not gradable as an ancient tradition (modern inference from A-texts).

### Tradition C — Paul reached Spain
- **Claim:** Paul fulfilled the plan of Rom 15:24, 28 and preached in Spain.
- **Sources, in order:**
  1. **Rom 15:24, 28** (A): *intention* only.
  2. **1 Clement 5.6–7** (c. 96, B): "After preaching both in the east and west … having taught righteousness to the whole world, and come to the extreme limit of the west (*epi to terma tēs dyseōs*), and suffered martyrdom under the prefects." ([New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1010.htm), Roberts–Donaldson tr.) **Ambiguous.** Written from Rome, the phrase could mean Rome itself, but in Greek and Latin geography it normally means the Pillars of Hercules or Spain. Scholars are divided.
  3. **Muratorian Fragment** lines 38–39 (B if late 2nd c.; C if 4th c. as argued by Sundberg and Hahneman): Luke omits "the departure of Paul from the city [of Rome] when he journeyed to Spain" ([earlychristianwritings.com](http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/muratorian-metzger.html), Metzger tr.).
  4. **Acts of Peter (Vercelli Acts) 1–3** (late 2nd c., D): the Lord in a vision says "Arise, Paul, and become a physician in thy body to them that are in Spain"; Paul leaves Rome amid weeping. ([earlychristianwritings.com](http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/actspeter.html), M. R. James tr.)
  5. Later Spanish local traditions (e.g. Tarragona — [Pleiades: Tarraco](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/246349)) have no early written source: **E**. Not researched in detail; UNVERIFIED.
- **Vreugdenhil** ch. 1: "and even went to Spain". He states it as fact.
- **Note for teachers:** this is the weakest point in Vreugdenhil's Paul narrative. Present it as "early tradition says…".

### Tradition D — Shipwreck location: Malta vs. alternatives
- **Majority view: Malta** (Melita, [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/462311)). The traditional site is St Paul's Bay. Acts 28:1 has *Melitē*, and the route Crete → Malta → Syracuse fits a gale from the NE (Acts 27:14).
- **Mljet, Croatia** (Melite/Meleda, [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/197376)). First proposed by Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus, *De administrando imperio* 36 (10th c.; chapter number UNVERIFIED). Grade **E** (late learned conjecture, based on Acts 27:27 "Adriatic"). Summary: [University of Malta repository](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/7810).
- **Kefalonia, Greece:** a modern proposal (H. Warnecke, 1987). Grade: modern hypothesis, not gradable. [HMML summary](https://hmml.org/stories/series-weather-contesting-saint-paul-s-shipwreck/).

### Tradition E — Birthplace: Tarsus vs. Gischala
- Tarsus: Acts 21:39; 22:3 (A).
- Gischala in Galilee (family origin; Paul moved to Tarsus as a child): Jerome, *Vir. ill.* 5 ("of the tribe of Benjamin and the town of Giscalis in Judea. When this was taken by the Romans he removed with his parents to Tarsus") (C). Jerome also mentions this in his *Commentary on Philemon* (v. 23) — UNVERIFIED. The two can be harmonised (family origin vs. place of birth).

### Place of execution
- **Rome:** B (1 Clement, Dionysius, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Gaius).
- **Aquae Salviae / Tre Fontane** on the Via Laurentina, c. 3 km beyond San Paolo (≈ 41.834, 12.484; no Pleiades entry found). The earliest source is the apocryphal *Passion of Peter and Paul* attributed to Pseudo-Marcellus (5th–7th c.): Paul was beheaded "at the Aquae Salviae, near the pine tree". The legend of three springs rising where the head bounced is later still. **D/E.** Pope Benedict XVI, General Audience 4 Feb 2009 ([vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2009/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20090204.html)) summarises this as the "most legendary" tradition.
- **Burial: Via Ostiensis** (Gaius c. 200, B; Jerome, C). The present basilica of San Paolo fuori le Mura stands over a Constantinian/Theodosian church on this spot. 2006 excavations; radiocarbon 1st–2nd c. on bone fragments (2009 papal announcement — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna31599704)). This supports an early cult site. It cannot prove whose bones they are.

### Vreugdenhil on Paul's death
- Ch. 1: "At Nero's command, the soldiers beheaded Paul and crucified Peter, probably upside down." Also: "when Paul was imprisoned and beheaded in Rome, John took his place and continued the work of Paul."
- Ch. 6 (*Nero the persecutor of the Christians*): mentions Peter's upside-down crucifixion but **does not mention Paul**.
- His account most likely rests on Eusebius *HE* 2.25 / Jerome *Vir. ill.* 5 (beheading under Nero) and Tertullian. Vreugdenhil gives no footnotes, so this is our inference.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **Conversion date (c. 31–36).** It depends on how the "three years" and "fourteen years" of Gal 1:18; 2:1 are counted, and on whether Gal 2 = Acts 15 or Acts 11. This is the largest uncertainty in the early timeline.
2. **Galatians: date and destination** (c. 48/49 vs. c. 53–55; South vs. North Galatia). This changes where it sits on the map and timeline.
3. **Festus' accession (55/56–61; most scholars 58–60).** It moves the arrest, the Caesarea imprisonment, the voyage and the arrival in Rome by up to ±2 years.
4. **Release or no release after Acts 28.** No first-century source states it explicitly. The case rests on 1 Clement 5.7 (ambiguous), the Muratorian Fragment (date disputed), Eusebius ("it is said"), and the Pastorals (authorship disputed). The map should present Spain and the post-Acts eastern journeys as **tradition**, not fact.
5. **Spain.** Vreugdenhil states it as fact. The evidence is grade A for intention, B (ambiguous) for the visit, D for the narrative. Flag this for teachers.
6. **Date of death: c. 62 / 64–65 / 67–68.** All are defended. Only "under Nero" (54–68) is solidly B-level. Jerome's "twenty-seventh year after the passion" conflicts with his own "fourteenth year of Nero". The manuscript readings were not verified.
7. **Provenance of the prison letters** (Rome vs. Ephesus vs. Caesarea). This affects which map pins they attach to.
8. **Malta vs. Mljet/Kefalonia.** Malta is the strong majority view. The alternatives are listed for completeness.
9. **Tre Fontane execution site:** a late (5th–7th c.) apocryphal source only.
10. **UNVERIFIED items to check before publishing:** Orosius 7.6.15 text; Seneca *Ep.* 104.1; Josephus *Ant.* 20.137–144 section numbers (Felix/Drusilla); Constantine VII *DAI* ch. 36; Jerome on Philemon (Gischala); Eusebius *Chronicle* year entry for Peter and Paul; the *Depositio martyrum* 29 June entry; page references in Brown, Murphy-O'Connor, Bruce and Warnecke; manuscript variant "37th year" in Jerome *Vir. ill.* 5.
