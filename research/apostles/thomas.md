# Thomas (Didymus, "the Twin"; Syriac: Judas Thomas)

Thomas is one of the Twelve. The Gospel of John tells us most about him: he was ready to die with Jesus, he asked "how can we know the way?", and he doubted the resurrection until he saw the risen Lord and confessed "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). After Acts 1:13 the New Testament says nothing more about him. Around AD 230 Origen recorded that Thomas was given Parthia (the Parthian empire, roughly modern Iran and Iraq) as his mission field. A later and more colourful story, the *Acts of Thomas* (early 3rd c.), sends him to "India" and to a king called Gundaphorus. Archaeology shows that a king with that name, Gondophares, really did rule in what is now Pakistan and Afghanistan around AD 20–46. The Christians of Kerala (South India) have a strong tradition that Thomas landed there in AD 52 and was killed near Chennai (Mylapore) in AD 72. That tradition has no early written source. From the 4th century on, his relics were venerated at Edessa (modern Şanlıurfa, Turkey).

## Competing traditions

### Tradition A — Parthia (Origen → Eusebius)
- **Claim:** Parthia "was allotted to Thomas as his field of labor."
- **Earliest source:** Origen, *Commentary on Genesis* bk 3 (c. 230; lost), quoted or summarised by Eusebius, *HE* 3.1.1–3 (c. 311–325). Scholars debate whether the phrase "These are the words of Origen" (3.1.3) covers the whole list or only the remarks on Peter and Paul. **Grade C.**
- **Later:** Rufinus, *HE* 10.9 (c. 402): Thomas to Parthia, Matthew to Ethiopia, Bartholomew to "India citerior". The Latin text is UNVERIFIED online here. It is relayed by Socrates, *HE* 1.19 (c. 440), which *is* verified: "Thomas received the apostleship of the Parthians" ([New Advent 26011](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26011.htm)). Ps-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* 8 (date uncertain, late antique or Byzantine) combines Traditions A and B: Thomas preached "to the Parthians, Medes, Persians, Hyrcanians, Bactrians, and Margians" and died "at Calamene, the city of India."
- **Note:** A and B are not necessarily contradictory. Gondophares' realm was *Indo-Parthian*, so in Roman terms "Parthia" and north-western "India" overlap. That reconciliation is a modern inference.

### Tradition B — "India" of King Gundaphorus (Acts of Thomas)
- **Claim:** Thomas is sold as a carpenter to the merchant of King Gundaphorus, preaches in "India", and is martyred under King Misdaeus/Mazdai.
- **Earliest source:** *Acts of Thomas* (Syriac original, probably Edessa, early 3rd c.; Harnack dated it before c. 220). It shows clear Gnostic/encratite features, and in ch. 31 it even makes Thomas Jesus' twin. **Grade D.**
- **Historical hook:** Gondophares is a real king (coins plus the Takht-i-Bahi inscription, regnal year 26 = AD 46 on the usual reckoning). J. F. Fleet argued that the names in the Acts "are distinctly not such as have lived in Indian story and tradition" (*JRAS* 1905, p. 235, cited in the Catholic Encyclopedia). The name may still have been borrowed for colour. The king proves that the author knew real Indo-Parthian history, not that Thomas went there.
- **Later:** Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration* 33 (§11 by the usual numbering, section number UNVERIFIED; c. 379–381): "Thomas with India" ([New Advent 310233](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310233.htm)). Ambrose, Jerome and Paulinus of Nola also mention it (Catholic Encyclopedia; individual references UNVERIFIED here). Gregory of Tours, *Glory of the Martyrs* 31–32 (c. 590), adds a church and a miraculous lamp in India at the original grave ([Oxford CSLA E00515](https://portal.sds.ox.ac.uk/articles/online_resource/E00515_Gregory_of_Tours_in_his_Glory_of_the_Martyrs_31_and_32_tells_of_the_body_of_Thomas_the_Apostle_S00199_being_translated_from_India_to_Edessa_Mesopotamia_how_an_oil_lamp_miraculously_burns_incessantly_in_his_church_in_India_and_how_var/13798907)).

### Tradition C — South India: Kerala (Muziris) and Mylapore
- **Claim:** Thomas lands at Kodungallur (Muziris) in AD 52 (some sources say 50), founds seven churches in Kerala, crosses to the east coast, and is martyred at Mylapore (Chennai) on 3 July 72.
- **Earliest source:** Local tradition of the Thomas Christians (Syriac-rite churches of Kerala). There is no written attestation before the medieval period. The Thomas Ramban Pattu is a late ballad, early-modern at the earliest. **Grade E.**
- **Supporting context (not proof):**
  - Christians in "Male" (Malabar?) under a bishop consecrated in Persia are attested by Cosmas Indicopleustes (6th c.). The reference to Cosmas, *Christian Topography* 3, is UNVERIFIED here.
  - A 7th-c. Pahlavi cross stands at St Thomas Mount (Catholic Encyclopedia).
  - The Roman trade route to Muziris was real and busy in the 1st c. (see row 12).
- **Scholarly position:** The Catholic Encyclopedia (1912) judged it "difficult to discover any adequate support" for Mylapore. Many modern historians regard a 1st-c. apostolic mission to South India as possible but unprovable. Indian church historians often defend it. There is no clear consensus.

### Note on the word "India"
Ancient "India" was elastic. It could mean the Indus/Gandhara region, the whole subcontinent, South Arabia, or even Aksum/Ethiopia. Socrates, *HE* 1.19, calls Frumentius' Aksum "India interior". So "Thomas in India" in a 4th-c. author is not automatically South India.

## Death / martyrdom

1. **Natural death (no martyrdom).** The Valentinian Heracleon (c. 170), quoted by Clement of Alexandria, *Stromata* 4.9 (c. 200), lists Thomas, together with Matthew, Philip and Levi, among those saved who "departed" *without* public confession before the authorities. Grade **B**: within ~150 years, but from a heterodox source, and Clement does not dispute the fact. [New Advent 02104](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02104.htm).
2. **Speared on a mountain under King Misdaeus/Mazdai, in an unnamed city in "India".** Four soldiers spear him. He is buried in the royal tomb, and the bones are later carried west. Grade **D**: *Acts of Thomas* 164–170. The Catholic Encyclopedia notes a suggestion that Mazdai = the Kushan king Vasudeva, which would be anachronistic for the 1st c.
3. **Speared with pine spears at "Calamene, the city of India"**, buried there. Grade **D**: Ps-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* 8. Pleiades/Barrington locate Calamina near the Coromandel coast ([Pleiades 49933](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/49933)), but that identification follows the later Mylapore tradition.
4. **Martyred at Mylapore / St Thomas Mount, 3 July AD 72.** Grade **E**: Kerala and Tamil tradition. The 3 July date is also Thomas' feast day in the Syriac churches and the modern Roman calendar.

**Vreugdenhil, ch. 1:** "Most likely, the savages in India murdered Thomas." His hedge ("most likely") is fair, but no ancient source has "savages". This rests on tradition 2/3/4. He does not mention tradition 1, which is the earliest.

## Open questions / uncertainties
- **Parthia vs India:** Origen's "Parthia" (C) is the oldest location claim. "India" (D) comes from a romance with a real king's name. Is "India" in the *Acts* the Indo-Parthian north-west rather than South India? Most scholars think so.
- **Gondophares' dates:** "AD 46" depends on identifying the "year 103" era with the Vikrama era. Coin studies suggest a reign of c. AD 19–46, but some numismatists propose different dates.
- **Kerala/Mylapore:** No written evidence before the medieval period. The dates (50 or 52 arrival, 72 death) come from late tradition and differ between versions.
- **Heracleon's "no martyrdom"** is the oldest statement on how Thomas died. It contradicts all the later traditions.
- **Ephrem CN 42:** quoted from a secondary translation. Check against Beck's CSCO edition before publication.
- **Rufinus HE 10.9:** verified only through Socrates' dependent text.
- **Vreugdenhil ch. 4** could not be checked (the download failed).
