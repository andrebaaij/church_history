# Matthias

Matthias is the apostle chosen to take the place of Judas Iscariot. Acts 1 tells how the
believers in Jerusalem put forward two men who had been with Jesus from his baptism by John
until the Ascension. They prayed and cast lots, and the lot fell on Matthias (Acts 1:15–26).
After that the Bible never mentions him by name again. Everything later writers say about
where he preached (Judea, "Ethiopia", which may mean Colchis in modern Georgia, the land of
the "man-eaters", even Britain) and how he died (stoned and beheaded in Jerusalem, crucified
in Colchis, or of old age) comes from sources written centuries afterwards, and these
sources contradict each other.

Out of scope for dating, but useful as an anchor: Clement of Alexandria (c. 200) is the earliest
writer to quote "Matthias" at all, and he quotes him only as a teacher of self-discipline. No
writer within about 150 years says where Matthias went or how he died.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition 1: One of the Seventy
- **Claim:** Matthias (and Joseph Barsabbas) belonged to the Seventy of Luke 10:1.
- **Earliest source:** Eusebius, HE 1.12.3 (c. 300–325): "Matthias, also, who was numbered with
  the apostles in the place of Judas, and the one who was honored by being made a candidate
  with him, are likewise said to have been deemed worthy of the same calling with the seventy."
  Eusebius says "are said", so it is hearsay. The nearby section 1.12.1–2 draws on Clement's
  *Hypotyposes*, but Eusebius does not attribute the Matthias claim to Clement.
- **Grade:** C.
- **Later:** Ps.-Hippolytus, *On the Seventy* §3; Ps.-Dorotheus, disciples' list §3.
- **Note:** This fits Acts 1:21–22, which requires a candidate who was with Jesus from John's baptism onward.

### Tradition 2: Judea / Jerusalem (preached, and died there)
There are two versions of his death here. See the next section.
- Ps.-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve Apostles* §12: "Matthias, who was one of the seventy, was
  numbered along with the eleven apostles, and preached in Jerusalem, and fell asleep and was
  buried there." This is a natural death. Grade D: a spurious, late Byzantine list of uncertain date.
- Golden Legend: he preached "about Jerusalem", was stoned, then killed with an axe. Grade D.

### Tradition 3: "Ethiopia", the city of the man-eaters (*Acts of Andrew and Matthias*)
- **Claim:** At the division of the world by lot, Matthias drew the "country of the man-eaters".
  He was seized there, blinded and imprisoned, and Andrew was sent to free him within three days.
- **Source:** *Acts of Andrew and Matthias*. It opens: "About that time all the apostles had
  come together to the same place, and shared among themselves the countries, casting lots…"
  (ANF 8; New Advent). The ANF text names no city. "Mermedonia" (often given as Myrmidonia) is
  the name used in the Old English poem *Andreas*. Some manuscripts read "Matthew" instead of
  "Matthias" (UNVERIFIED; this is commonly stated in the secondary literature, but I did not check it against a critical edition).
- **Grade:** D.
- **Note:** The text says nothing about his death.

### Tradition 4: "Ethiopia" = Colchis (Georgia), ending with crucifixion
- **Claim:** He preached first in Judea, then in "Ethiopia, that is to say, Colchis", and was crucified.
- **Earliest source located:** Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos, *Historia ecclesiastica* 2.40
  (c. 1320). Cited from the Catholic Encyclopedia; the primary text (PG 145) is UNVERIFIED online.
- **Grade:** D (a 14th-century writer with no traceable older source). The tomb at Gonio is E.
- **Local tradition:** A marker in the Roman fort at Gonio-Apsaros (Adjara, Georgia) claims the
  apostle's grave. No excavation has confirmed it (Wikipedia, *Gonio*; georgia.travel).

### Tradition 5: Pseudo-Dorotheus of Tyre (two divergent versions)
- **Version in the Catholic Encyclopedia:** He preached "to barbarians and cannibals in the
  interior of Ethiopia, at the harbour of the sea of Hyssus, at the mouth of the river Phasis.
  He died at Sebastopolis, and was buried there, near the Temple of the Sun." Geographically
  this mixes Hyssos (Sürmene, Turkey) with the mouth of the Phasis (Poti, Georgia), about 200 km
  apart. Sebastopolis is probably Dioscurias (Sukhumi), but this is my identification, not the source's.
- **Version in Schermann's edition (as translated on NASSCAL e-Clavis):** "Matthias, who was
  numbered with the eleven apostles in place of Judas Iscariot, after preaching Christ in
  Ethiopia Prima, later he was crucified in Britain."
- **Date:** The compilation dates from the 8th century, and certainly before 810/811 (NASSCAL).
- **Grade:** D.

### Tradition 6: Cappadocia and the Caspian coast
- **Claim:** He spread Christianity in Cappadocia and along the Caspian Sea, living mainly near port Hyssus.
- **Source:** Wikipedia calls it "Greek tradition" without naming a primary text. UNVERIFIED.
- **Grade:** E until a source is found.

### Tradition 7: Matthias as a teacher (Clement) and as a claimed source for Gnostics
- Clement of Alexandria (c. 200) quotes sayings attributed to Matthias:
  - *Strom.* 2.9.45: "Wonder at what is before you". ANF prints "Matthew".
  - *Strom.* 3.4.26: fight the flesh and strengthen the soul. The ANF text here is in Latin, and the name is "Matthiam".
  - *Strom.* 7.13.82: "if the neighbour of an elect man sin, the elect man has sinned". ANF prints "Matthew the apostle".
  - *Strom.* 7.17.108: the Basilideans "boast of adducing the opinion of Matthew [Matthias]".
  - *Strom.* 4.6.35: Zacchaeus, "or, according to some, Matthew". Scholars often read this as Matthias (earlychristianwritings.com lists it as possibly from the same source). The Greek reading is UNVERIFIED.

  Grade B for the claim that Clement knew such sayings. Their authenticity as the apostle's words
  is not established, and the *Traditions of Matthias* is usually dated c. 110–160
  (earlychristianwritings.com).
- Eusebius, HE 3.29.4 repeats Clement's 3.4 passage ("They say that Matthias also taught…").
  HE 3.25.6 lists a "Gospel of Matthias" among heretical writings. Origen, *Hom. in Luc.* 1 (c. 233)
  is the earliest writer to name a Gospel of Matthias. That reference comes from secondary
  sources; the exact wording is UNVERIFIED.
- Hippolytus, *Refutation* 7.20.1 (ANF 7.8): "Basilides… and Isidorus… say that Matthias
  communicated to them secret discourses, which… he heard from the Saviour." Hippolytus calls
  this a lie against Matthias and against the Saviour. Grade B for the claim having been made.

## Death / martyrdom

No source written within 150 years of his life gives his death, its place, or its date. **No
source gives a year.** The accounts below conflict and are listed separately.

| Account | Place | Manner | Earliest source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Stoned, then beheaded | Jerusalem / Judea | Condemned by the high priest and stoned; when the stoning did not kill him, he was beheaded with an axe "after the manner of the Romans" | Jacobus de Voragine, *Legenda aurea* (c. 1260), citing a legend "found at Treves", in Caxton's 1483 English; Tillemont via the Catholic Encyclopedia | D |
| B. Crucified in Colchis | Colchis ("Ethiopia"); Gonio / Apsaros by local tradition | Crucifixion | Nicephorus Callistus, HE 2.40 (c. 1320; UNVERIFIED in the primary text) | D (tomb: E) |
| C. Died and buried at Sebastopolis | Sebastopolis (probably Sukhumi) | Not stated (natural?) | Ps.-Dorotheus (Catholic Encyclopedia version; 8th c.) | D |
| D. Crucified in Britain | Britain | Crucifixion | Ps.-Dorotheus (Schermann text via NASSCAL) | D |
| E. Natural death in Jerusalem | Jerusalem | "Fell asleep and was buried there" | Ps.-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* §12 | D |

**Vreugdenhil** (ch. 1, *The Apostolic Age*) gives only account A: "Mattias was first stoned and
then beheaded." The ancient basis for his version is the medieval Western legend in the Golden
Legend, not any early church writer. Teachers should know that this is the latest kind of
source in this file.

The Catholic Encyclopedia notes that Bollandus suspected the relic and death stories may confuse
the apostle with a later **Matthias, bishop of Jerusalem (c. 120)**.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **No early source gives his mission field or his death.** Everything after Acts 1 comes from the
   4th century at the earliest (and the *Acts of Andrew and Matthias* is itself of uncertain date).
   For a map, only Jerusalem (rows 3–5) is grade A.
2. **Matthias or Matthew?** Manuscripts confuse the names, both in Clement (ANF prints "Matthew")
   and in the *Acts of Andrew and Matthias*. Whether a given passage is "really" about Matthias
   depends on the critical edition. I have not verified the Greek of *Strom.* 2.9.45, 4.6.35,
   7.13.82 or 7.17.108.
3. **The Pseudo-Dorotheus text differs by recension.** One version has Sebastopolis and the
   Temple of the Sun; another has Ethiopia Prima and crucifixion in Britain. Which is older?
4. **Where is "Ethiopia"?** Equating it with Colchis is a Byzantine harmonisation (Nicephorus).
   Sebastopolis = Sukhumi is my identification and is not stated in any source.
5. **Nicephorus HE 2.40 and Tillemont** were only checked through the Catholic Encyclopedia. The
   primary texts are UNVERIFIED.
6. The "Cappadocia / Caspian / port Hyssus" tradition has no primary source identified. UNVERIFIED.
7. **Possible confusion with Bishop Matthias of Jerusalem (c. 120),** raised by Bollandus via the Catholic Encyclopedia.
8. Vreugdenhil's "stoned then beheaded" rests on the 13th-century Golden Legend. His wording is
   closest to Foxe, *Book of Martyrs* ch. 1 §VI (1563): "He was stoned at Jerusalem and then
   beheaded" (https://www.ccel.org/f/foxe/martyrs/fox101.htm, checked). Foxe is the likely
   channel through which this tradition reached the Protestant retelling. Teachers may
   want to present it as "a medieval tradition says…".
9. Vreugdenhil ch. 4 was not checked because the PDF did not download.
