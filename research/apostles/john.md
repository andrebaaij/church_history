# John son of Zebedee (the apostle John)

Summary (youth group): John was a Galilean fisherman, the younger brother of James, and
one of Jesus' three closest disciples. After Pentecost he worked in Jerusalem alongside
Peter, and Paul counted him one of the three "pillars" of the church there. Christian
writers of the 2nd century tell us he later lived in Ephesus (in modern Turkey), was
banished to the island of Patmos, where he received the visions of the book of
Revelation, and returned to Ephesus, where he died of old age under the emperor Trajan,
around AD 100 — the last of the apostles. Not everything is certain: the date of
Revelation is debated (Domitian c. 95 is the oldest and majority view, Nero c. 68 a
minority one), and some early sources speak of a second "John the Elder" at Ephesus.

> **Map anchor:** the end year of the map (c. 100) rests on Irenaeus (c. 180), *Against
> Heresies* 2.22.5 and 3.3.4: John "remained among them up to the times of Trajan"
> (Trajan reigned 98–117). That is grade B, not A. See "Death" and "Open questions".

## Competing traditions

### 1. When was John on Patmos / when was Revelation written?

**1a — Under Domitian, c. 95 (majority view)**
- Earliest source: Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.3 (c. 180): the apocalyptic vision
  "was seen no very long time since, but almost in our day, towards the end of
  Domitian's reign." Grade B.
  - Grammatical note: some early-date advocates argue the subject of "was seen" is John
    (not the vision); most commentators read it as the vision (see Swete 1906, ch. IX).
- Later sources: Victorinus of Pettau, *Commentary on the Apocalypse* on 10:11 (late
  3rd c.): John "was in the island of Patmos, condemned to the labour of the mines by
  Caesar Domitian… Domitian being killed… John being dismissed from the mines". Grade C.
  Eusebius *HE* 3.18.1–3 (quotes Irenaeus), 3.20.8–11, 3.23.1 (C). Jerome *De vir. ill.*
  9: "In the fourteenth year… Domitian having raised a second persecution he was
  banished to the island of Patmos" (C).
- Supporting context: Tertullian, *Apology* 5 (197): Domitian "tried his hand at
  persecution… even restoring again those whom he had banished" (does not name John).
  Eusebius *HE* 3.18.4–5: Flavia Domitilla exiled in Domitian's 15th year.
- Scholarly status (reference, not graded): Swete (1906): "Early Christian tradition is
  practically unanimous in assigning the Apocalypse to the last years of Domitian."
  Kümmel, *Introduction to the NT*: the scene of Revelation "fits no epoch of primitive
  Christianity so well as the time of the persecution under Domitian." This is the
  majority scholarly view.

**1b — Exile by an unnamed emperor (consistent with either date)**
- Clement of Alexandria, *Who is the Rich Man* 42 (c. 200): returned to Ephesus "on the
  tyrant's death" — tyrant not named. Grade B.
- Origen, *Commentary on Matthew* 16.6 (c. 246–249): "the king of the Romans, as
  tradition teaches, condemned John… to the island of Patmos" — not named. Grade C
  (Greek text via Swete 1906; no free English translation found).
- Tertullian, *Prescription* 36: oil at Rome, then "his island-exile" — no emperor named.

**1c — Under Nero, c. 64–68 (minority view)**
- No early church writer names Nero. Earliest source: the title of the Syriac
  (Philoxenian 508 / Harklean 616) version of Revelation: "The Revelation which was made
  to John the Evangelist by God in the Island Patmos, into which he was banished by Nero
  the Caesar" (as quoted by Newton, 1733; printed by de Dieu, Leiden 1627). The title is
  a later addition of uncertain date. Grade D/C. Note: Revelation was not part of the
  Peshitta canon, so "Peshitta" is the wrong label.
- Main basis is internal: the temple apparently still standing (Rev 11:1–2); the
  "seven kings" (Rev 17:9–11) counted from Augustus or Julius; 666 (Rev 13:18) as the
  Hebrew gematria of "Nero Caesar". These are interpretations of A-grade text, not
  testimony.
- Modern proponents (reference): J.A.T. Robinson, *Redating the New Testament* (1976);
  K.L. Gentry, *Before Jerusalem Fell* (1989). Popular in preterist interpretation.
- Relevance for the map: a Neronian date would put John on Patmos c. 65–68 and possibly
  in Asia before 70.

**1d — Under Claudius (41–54)**
- Epiphanius, *Panarion* 51.12.2 and 51.33.9 (374–377): John "prophesied in the times of
  Claudius Caesar… when he was on the island of Patmos", returning under Claudius.
  Grade C. Generally regarded as an error (Swete). Greek checked via Swete 1906 (whose
  numbering gives 51.32 for 51.33); the Williams English translation is not freely
  available.

**1e — Under Trajan (98–117)**
- Theophylact of Ohrid (11th c.), on Matt 20:22: "Trajan condemned John". Pseudo-
  Dorotheus similar. Grade C/D (late). Swete suggests a misreading of Irenaeus' "until
  the times of Trajan". Primary text (PG 123) not opened — via Swete.

### 2. John the apostle and "John the Elder"

**2a — One John: the apostle = "the disciple of the Lord" at Ephesus = author of Gospel,
letters and Revelation**
- Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 81.4 (c. 155–160; dialogue set at Ephesus per
  Eusebius *HE* 4.18.6): "a certain man with us, whose name was John, one of the
  apostles of Christ, who prophesied, by a revelation that was made to him…" — the
  earliest attribution of Revelation to the apostle. Grade B.
- Irenaeus, *AH* 3.1.1 (c. 180): "John, the disciple of the Lord, who also had leaned
  upon His breast, did himself publish a Gospel during his residence at Ephesus." Grade
  B. Irenaeus also calls Papias "a hearer of John" (Eusebius *HE* 3.39.1).
- Polycrates of Ephesus (c. 190, in *HE* 3.31.3; 5.24.3): "John, who was both a witness
  and a teacher, who reclined upon the bosom of the Lord, and, being a priest, wore the
  sacerdotal plate… He fell asleep at Ephesus." Grade B. (He does not use the word
  "apostle" for John here, though he does for Philip.)
- Note: Irenaeus' anchoring passages (2.22.5; 3.3.4) call him "John, the disciple of
  the Lord"; Eusebius (*HE* 3.23.1) calls the same person "the apostle and evangelist".

**2b — Two Johns at Ephesus: the apostle and "the presbyter (elder) John"**
- Papias of Hierapolis (*Expositions of the Oracles of the Lord*, c. 110–130), preface,
  quoted in Eusebius *HE* 3.39.4: he asked "what Andrew or what Peter said, or… James,
  or… John, or… Matthew… and what things Aristion and the presbyter John, the disciples
  of the Lord, say." Grade B (the quotation). The name John appears twice.
- Eusebius' interpretation (*HE* 3.39.5–7, c. 313–325): the second John is a different
  person, "outside the number of the apostles"; Papias heard Aristion and the presbyter
  John, not the apostles; "there were two tombs in Ephesus, each of which… is called
  John's"; perhaps the Elder saw the Revelation. Grade C. (Eusebius disliked
  Revelation's millennialism, which may colour this suggestion.)
- Dionysius of Alexandria (c. 250, in Eusebius *HE* 7.25.7–16): on grounds of style,
  Revelation is by a different John than the Gospel and 1 John; "they say that there
  are two monuments in Ephesus, each bearing the name of John". Grade C.
- Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 9 (392): 2 and 3 John "are said to be the work of John the
  presbyter to the memory of whom another sepulchre is shown at Ephesus to the present
  day, though some think that there are two memorials of this same John the evangelist."
  Grade C.
- NT data: the author of 2 John and 3 John calls himself "the elder" (2 John 1; 3 John
  1). The author of Revelation calls himself only "John" (Rev 1:1, 4, 9; 22:8). The
  Gospel is attributed to "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (John 21:20–24) and does not
  name him. Grade A for the wording; the identifications are not stated.
- Modern positions (reference, not graded): M. Hengel, *The Johannine Question* (1989),
  and R. Bauckham, *Jesus and the Eyewitnesses* (2006), argue that John the Elder was
  the beloved disciple and evangelist, distinct from John son of Zebedee (Bauckham's
  chapter numbers UNVERIFIED). Many conservative scholars hold that Papias' two
  mentions both refer to the apostle (no specific citation verified — UNVERIFIED).
- **Relevance for the map:** if the long-lived John of Ephesus was the Elder and not the
  son of Zebedee, the c. 100 end-date still marks the death of the last
  "disciple of the Lord" in Irenaeus' sense, but not necessarily of an apostle of the
  Twelve. The map caption could say "John (identified by Irenaeus with the apostle)".

### 3. Mary at Ephesus (outside scope, for awareness)
- John 19:27 (A) says the beloved disciple took Mary "to his own home" — no place
  given. The "House of Mary" (Meryem Ana Evi) near Ephesus rests on 19th-century
  visions (Anne Catherine Emmerich) and local tradition; a rival tradition places
  Mary's death in Jerusalem. Grade E. No sources researched in depth here.

## Death / martyrdom

### Account 1 — Natural death in old age at Ephesus, under Trajan (c. 98–100+)
- Earliest sources:
  - Irenaeus, *AH* 2.22.5 (c. 180): "those who were conversant in Asia with John, the
    disciple of the Lord… And he remained among them up to the times of Trajan." Grade B.
  - Irenaeus, *AH* 3.3.4: "the Church in Ephesus, founded by Paul, and having John
    remaining among them permanently until the times of Trajan, is a true witness of the
    tradition of the apostles." Grade B.
  - Polycrates of Ephesus, *Letter to Victor of Rome* (c. 190), in Eusebius *HE* 3.31.3
    and 5.24.3: John "fell asleep at Ephesus". Grade B (Polycrates was bishop of Ephesus
    and claims family tradition).
- Later sources: Eusebius *HE* 3.1.1 (Origen), 3.23.1–4, 3.31 (C); Jerome *De vir. ill.*
  9: "continuing there until the time of the Emperor Trajan… worn out by old age, died
  in the sixty-eighth year after our Lord's passion and was buried near the same city"
  (C). (The New Advent translation of Jerome reads "returned to Ephesus under Pertinax"
  — an error in the text or translation for Nerva.)
- Date basis: Trajan became emperor on 28 January 98 (Nerva's death); Irenaeus gives no
  year. Jerome's 68th year after the Passion gives c. 98 (from a Passion in 30) to c. 101
  (from 33). Range: **98 – c. 100**, conventionally "c. 100". Nothing excludes a later
  year in Trajan's reign (to 117) except John's great age.
- Vreugdenhil ch. 1 follows this account ("quietly and peacefully entered eternal
  glory").

### Account 2 — The "metastasis": John lies down in his own grave (apocryphal)
- *Acts of John* 106–115 (2nd–3rd c.; M.R. James tr. 1924): at Ephesus John has his
  disciples dig a trench ("Let the trench be deeper"), lies down in it, says "Peace be
  with you, brethren", and "gave up his spirit rejoicing". Grade D.
- Augustine, *Tractates on John* 124.2 (c. 420), reports (sceptically) a story that
  John is "sleeping rather than lying dead in his tomb at Ephesus", the earth heaving
  "by his breathing". Grade C (report) / E (legend). Rooted in the rumour of John
  21:22–23 ("that this disciple was not to die"), which the Gospel itself corrects
  (A).

### Account 3 — Martyred ("killed by the Jews"), possibly together with James
- Philip of Side (c. 430s), epitome in Codex Baroccianus 142: "Papias in his second
  book says that John the Divine and James his brother were killed by the Jews."
  Grade C (5th-c. report of a 2nd-c. book; the Papias original is lost).
- George Hamartolos, *Chronicle* (9th c.; an interpolated form, Codex Coislinianus 305):
  after Nerva recalled John from Patmos, "Papias… says that he was killed by the Jews,
  and thereby evidently fulfilled, together with his brother, Christ's prophecy"; adds
  that Origen affirmed John was martyred. Grade C/D.
- Syriac martyrology (manuscript dated 411, BL Add. 12150), 27 December: "John and
  Jacob (James), the Apostles, at Jerusalem." Grade C. Commemorates the brothers
  together, which some read as implying both were martyred.
- Mark 10:39: "the cup that I drink you will drink" — a prophecy that both brothers
  would suffer; does not say how (A text; interpretation disputed).
- Notes: most scholars treat the Papias report as garbled or as referring to a
  different event; it contradicts Irenaeus and Polycrates (B). If taken at face value
  it could place John's death in Jerusalem before 70 (with James, 44, or later). We
  list it without deciding.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **End year of the map.** "c. 100" rests on Irenaeus' "until the times of Trajan"
   (B) and Jerome's "68th year after the Passion" (C). Defensible range 98–c. 100; a
   later year under Trajan is not excluded.
2. **Date of Revelation / Patmos.** Domitian c. 95 (Irenaeus; majority) vs Nero c. 68
   (internal arguments + late Syriac title; minority) vs Claudius (Epiphanius) vs
   Trajan (Theophylact). Map default: c. 95, with a note.
3. **Which John lived at Ephesus?** Papias' "presbyter John" (Eusebius *HE* 3.39)
   leaves open that the long-lived Ephesian John was not the son of Zebedee.
4. **Martyrdom report** (Papias via Philip of Side / George Hamartolos) contradicts
   Irenaeus and Polycrates; likely garbled but cannot be dismissed outright.
5. **When John moved to Asia** is unknown; Vreugdenhil's "after Paul's death" is
   inference, not ancient testimony.
6. **Vreugdenhil's "probably Smyrna"** for the robber story has no basis in Clement,
   who leaves the city unnamed; source for Smyrna not found.
7. **Grammar of Irenaeus 5.30.3** ("it/he was seen") is used by early-date advocates;
   majority reads it as the vision.
8. Not opened / via secondary source only: Origen *Comm. Matt.* 16.6, Epiphanius
   (Williams tr.), Theophylact (PG 123), Brown's date wording, Bauckham chapter
   numbers.
