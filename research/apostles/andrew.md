# Andrew

Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, was a fisherman from Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee. He was first a disciple of John the Baptist and was one of the first two people to follow Jesus. He then brought his brother Simon to Jesus. The New Testament mentions him for the last time in the upper room in Jerusalem after the Ascension (Acts 1:13). Everything after that comes from later writers. Origen (3rd century) says Andrew's mission field was "Scythia", the lands north of the Black Sea. The apocryphal *Acts of Andrew* (late 2nd century) says he was martyred at Patras in Greece. The X-shaped "St Andrew's cross" is a medieval addition: no early source mentions it. Stories of him founding the church of Byzantium (Constantinople) or visiting Kyiv come from much later.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition 1: Scythia (north of the Black Sea)
- **Claim:** Andrew's allotted mission field was Scythia.
- **Earliest source:** Origen, *Commentary on Genesis* III (c. 230s), lost, quoted in Eusebius, *HE* 3.1.1: "Parthia, according to tradition, was allotted to Thomas as his field of labor, Scythia to Andrew, and Asia to John". Grade C: Eusebius quotes Origen, and Origen himself says "according to tradition".
- **Later:** Pseudo-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* 2 ("Scythians and Thracians"); Pseudo-Dorotheus (8th c.: "coastal cities of Bithynia and also Pontus, Thrace and Scythia … Sebastopolis Major").
- **Notes:** "Scythia" is vague. It can mean the steppe from the Danube to the Don. Modern national traditions place it in Romania (Scythia Minor/Dobruja) or Ukraine/Russia (Kyiv). Neither identification is in Origen.

### Tradition 2: Achaia / Patras (death and burial)
- **Claim:** Andrew worked in Achaia and was crucified at Patras under the proconsul Aegeates.
- **Earliest source:** *Acts of Andrew*, an apocryphal novel. Most scholars date it c. 150–200; D.R. MacDonald argues for late-2nd-century Alexandria. Original Greek reconstructed by J.-M. Prieur (CCSA 5–6, 1989) and MacDonald (1990). Grade D. Eusebius (*HE* 3.25.6) lists the *Acts of Andrew* among heretical books "which no one belonging to the succession of ecclesiastical writers has deemed worthy of mention".
- **Later church writers:** Jerome, *Ep.* 59.5 (395/396; Achaia); Gaudentius of Brescia, *Sermon* 17 (c. 402; Patras); Pseudo-Hippolytus 2 (Patrae, "crucified, suspended on an olive tree … and there too he was buried"); Pseudo-Dorotheus (buried at Patras). Grade C/D.
- **Notes:** The 357 transfer of relics "from Achaia" to Constantinople (row 14) shows the Patras tradition was established by the mid-4th century.

### Tradition 3: Epirus
- **Source:** Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration* 33.11 (Constantinople, c. 379/80), lists apostles with their regions: "Luke with Achaia, Andrew with Epirus". Grade C.
- **Notes:** This stands apart from the Achaia/Patras tradition and gives Achaia to Luke.

### Tradition 4: Black Sea / Asia Minor itinerary
- **Source:** *Acts of Andrew* as epitomised by Gregory of Tours (585–594, Tours). The route runs from Pontus via Amasia, Sinope, Nicaea, Nicomedia, Byzantium, Thrace, Perinthus, Philippi and Thessalonica to Patras. Grade D.
- **Related:** *Acts of Andrew and Matthias* ("city of the cannibals"), D.

### Tradition 5: Founder of the church of Byzantium
- **Claim:** Andrew preached at Byzantium and ordained Stachys (the "Stachys" of Rom 16:9) as its first bishop. Later lists give AD 38 (the date's source is UNVERIFIED in this session).
- **Earliest source:** Pseudo-Dorotheus of Tyre, *List of the Apostles and Disciples*, compiled probably in the 8th century, certainly before 810/811 (e-Clavis). Grade D/E.
- **Scholarship:** F. Dvornik, *The Idea of Apostolicity in Byzantium and the Legend of the Apostle Andrew* (Harvard, 1958). As summarised in a review, Dvornik concludes that the legend was codified in Byzantium only in the 9th century and generally accepted there only in the 10th. It served to give Constantinople an apostolic founder comparable to Peter at Rome. (Review summary only; Dvornik's text was not checked in this session.)
- **Notes:** Andrew's passage through Byzantium in the *Acts of Andrew* (row 9) and the 357 relic transfer (row 14) are earlier "hooks" for the later legend.

### Tradition 6: Kyiv and Novgorod
- **Source:** *Russian Primary Chronicle* (compiled c. 1113 in Kyiv). Andrew travels from Sinope to Chersonesos (Korsun) in Crimea, up the Dnieper to the hills of Kyiv, where he erects a cross, and on to the Slovenes near Novgorod. Grade E (a legend first written more than a thousand years after the events).

### Tradition 7: Scythia Minor (Dobruja, Romania)
- **Source:** Modern Romanian Orthodox tradition: a cave near Ion Corvin, Constanța county, and 20th-century folk tales. It is based on Origen's "Scythia" read as Scythia Minor (the Roman province around Tomis/Constanța). Grade E.
- **Notes:** Some Romanian scholars regard the claim of 1st-century Christianisation as unsustainable and link its promotion to "protochronist" ideology (see Wikipedia, "Saint Andrew in Romania", and the Romanian Patriarchate's own presentation at basilica.ro).

### Tradition 8: Andrew and the writing of John's Gospel
- **Source:** Muratorian Fragment, lines 9–16. John's fellow-disciples fast for three days, and "it was revealed to Andrew, one of the apostles, that John should write down all things in his own name". The date is disputed: traditionally late 2nd century (B), but Sundberg and Hahneman argue for the 4th century (C). Grade B/C.
- **Notes:** It implies that Andrew was alive when John's Gospel was written, which is chronologically doubtful.

## Death / martyrdom

**Year:** No ancient source gives a year. Popular summaries say "c. 60" or "under Nero", but no early basis was found (UNVERIFIED). The feast is kept on 30 November; the earliest source for that date was not checked in this session (UNVERIFIED).

### Account A: Crucified at Patras, tied with ropes, on an ordinary cross
- *Acts of Andrew* (c. 150–200), Passion section. The proconsul Aegeates has Andrew flogged and crucified, tied with ropes rather than nailed so that he suffers longer. Andrew preaches from the cross for days before dying. Grade D. (Shape of the cross not specified in the summaries consulted.)
- Gregory of Tours, *Lib. de mir.* 35–38 (585–594), a brief martyrdom section. D.

### Account B: Crucified upright on an olive tree at Patras
- Pseudo-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve Apostles* 2: "crucified, suspended on an olive tree, at Patrae, a town of Achaia; and there too he was buried." Grade D (late, falsely attributed).

### Account C: The X-shaped cross ("saltire")
- **Not in the *Acts of Andrew* or any early source.** The X-shaped cross is a medieval development. Its familiar iconography is usually dated to the 10th–14th centuries; one popular account places the earliest securely dated use in Scotland c. 1180 (William I's seal). Exact first instance UNVERIFIED; see the National Gallery (London) glossary entry "Saint Andrew".
- Grade E (iconographic tradition).
- **Vreugdenhil** (ch. 1) tells this version ("a rough cross made of two beams nailed together in the centre … 'St. Andrew's cross'"). No ancient source supports it, so teachers should present it as later tradition.

### Relics (anchor for the Patras tradition)
- 356/357: Constantius II moves the remains from Achaia to the Church of the Holy Apostles, Constantinople (Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 7; Philostorgius, *HE* 3.2). Grade C.
- Later relic history (Amalfi 1208; partial return to Patras 1964; St Andrews legend in Scotland) falls outside the AD 30–100 scope and was not researched in detail.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **Nothing after Acts 1:13 is NT-based.** Every later location is C at best, and the best (Origen's "Scythia") is a vague region.
2. **Scythia vs Achaia vs Epirus:** three separate 3rd–4th-century traditions. They can be harmonised as a route (Scythia → Thrace → Greece), as later lists do, but no early source gives the route except the apocryphal *Acts of Andrew* (D).
3. **Year of death:** unknown. Any year on the map (e.g. "c. 60") would be invented. Better to show "1st century, date unknown".
4. **X-shaped cross:** Vreugdenhil's version is medieval. The earliest securely dated instance needs a proper art-historical citation (UNVERIFIED).
5. **Byzantium/Stachys and the "AD 38" date** are 8th–9th-century constructions (Dvornik). The source of "38" was not traced.
6. **The Muratorian Fragment's date** (2nd vs 4th century) affects the grade of the Andrew–John story.
7. **30 November:** the earliest attestation of the feast date was not traced.
8. The national traditions (Romania, Ukraine/Russia, Georgia, Scotland) are later. Georgia and Scotland were not researched; the others are listed as grade E only.
