# Matthew (also called Levi, son of Alphaeus)

Matthew was a tax collector at Capernaum. Jesus called him with the words "Follow me", and Matthew held a feast for "publicans and sinners" in his house. After Acts 1:13 the New Testament says nothing more about him. The earliest Christian writers (Papias, c. 110–130; Irenaeus, c. 180) say that Matthew wrote a Gospel "in the Hebrew language" for Jewish believers. Later writers (Rufinus and Socrates, c. 400–440) say that his mission field was "Ethiopia". Other texts say Parthia, or a "city of man-eaters" near the Black Sea. A very early (but heterodox) witness says he did **not** die as a martyr. In the ancient sources "Ethiopia" could mean the kingdom of Kush/Meroë (modern Sudan), the kingdom of Aksum (modern Ethiopia/Eritrea), or even an "Ethiopia" in Asia. Each source therefore has to be read carefully.

**Bottom line for the map:** draw Matthew's "Ethiopia" as **Kush/Meroë** (most sources), and mark Aksum and the Caspian/Pontus "Ethiopia" as alternative readings. Do not merge Kush and Aksum. In the 1st century they were separate kingdoms.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition A — Ethiopia
- **Claim:** Matthew's lot was Ethiopia, where he preached and (in later versions) was martyred.
- **Earliest source:** Rufinus, *HE* 10.9 (c. 402), relayed in Socrates, *HE* 1.19. **Grade C.**
- **Later:** *Passio Matthaei* (Naddaver; King Egippus/Hirtacus in the Latin legend, primary text UNVERIFIED here) (D). The Roman Martyrology: "S. Matthaei, qui in Aethiopia praedicans martyrium passus est" (quoted in the Catholic Encyclopedia).
- **Note:** The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo tradition is UNVERIFIED in this file.

### Tradition B — Myrna, "city of the man-eaters" (Pontus?)
- **Claim:** Matthew preaches among cannibals (with Andrew), converts King Fulvanus' household, and dies there.
- **Earliest source:** *Acts and Martyrdom of St. Matthew* (Greek). Lipsius dated it to the 3rd c. and noted Gnostic traces (per the Catholic Encyclopedia). **Grade D.** The Catholic Encyclopedia calls it "absolutely devoid of historic value".
- **Note:** The related *Acts of Andrew and Matthias* has Matthias, in some versions Matthew, in the cannibal city. Name confusion between Matthew and Matthias is common in these texts.

### Tradition C — Parthia
- **Claim:** Matthew died ("fell asleep") at Hierees in Parthia.
- **Earliest source:** Ps-Hippolytus, *On the Twelve* 7 (date uncertain). **Grade D.** Other Latin witnesses (Ambrose? Paulinus of Nola, *Carmen* 19?) are UNVERIFIED.

### Tradition D — Persia, Macedonia, Syria
Listed by the Catholic Encyclopedia without primary references. **UNVERIFIED.**

## Death / martyrdom
1. **Natural death, no martyrdom.** Heracleon the Valentinian (c. 170), quoted by Clement of Alexandria (*Stromata* 4.9), names "Matthew, Philip, Thomas, Levi, and many others" among the saved who did not make a confession before the authorities. **Grade B.** This is the *earliest* statement about Matthew's death, but it comes from a heterodox teacher. Clement disputes Heracleon's theology but not this fact. Note that Heracleon lists **Matthew and Levi separately**: he did not treat them as one person. [New Advent 02104](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02104.htm)
2. **"Fell asleep" (natural death) at Hierees, Parthia.** **D**: Ps-Hippolytus 7.
3. **Martyred in Ethiopia** while preaching (manner unspecified: burned, stoned, or beheaded in different versions). **D/C**: *Passio Matthaei*; Roman Martyrology. The Catholic Encyclopedia: "it is not known whether he was burned, stoned, or beheaded."
4. **Death in Myrna (Pontus?)** in the *Acts and Martyrdom of St. Matthew*. **D.**

Relic anchor (outside scope): Salerno Cathedral claims Matthew's relics, translated there in 954. The primary source is UNVERIFIED. Salernum: [Pleiades 433075](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/433075).

**Vreugdenhil, ch. 1:** "Bartholomew, Matthew, Simon the Zealot and Thaddeus all died the martyrs death." He does not mention the older Heracleon tradition (natural death) or where the martyrdom took place.

## Open questions / uncertainties
- **Matthew = Levi?** The Gospels equate them (Matt 9:9 vs Mark 2:14). Heracleon lists them separately, so some early readers did not.
- **Date of the Gospel:** Irenaeus implies the 60s. Most modern scholars date canonical Matthew c. 80–90, and many doubt apostolic authorship. Conservative scholars hold to the 50s–60s. Papias' "Hebrew oracles" may not be our Greek Matthew.
- **Which "Ethiopia"?** Kush/Meroë is best supported. The Catholic Encyclopedia's "south of the Caspian" has no ancient source traced. **This needs a source before it goes on the map.**
- **The "fifteen years in Judea"** claim is untraced (UNVERIFIED).
- **Death:** the earliest witness (Heracleon, B) says natural death. All martyrdom accounts are D-grade legends.
- **Rufinus 10.9 Latin** has not been checked directly. **Salerno 954** has not been checked against a primary source.
