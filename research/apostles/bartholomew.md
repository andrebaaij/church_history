# Bartholomew (Bar-Talmai, "son of Talmai"; often identified with Nathanael)

Bartholomew is one of the Twelve, but the New Testament gives only his name in the lists of apostles. Many Christians think he is the same person as Nathanael, the man from Cana whom Jesus called "an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile" (John 1:47). That identification is an inference, first made explicitly in the Middle Ages. The oldest tradition about his work comes from Eusebius (c. 320). Around AD 180–190 the Alexandrian teacher Pantaenus travelled to "India" and found Christians there who had a Hebrew copy of Matthew's Gospel, left behind by Bartholomew. Which "India" this was (South Arabia, Ethiopia/Aksum, or India proper) is unclear. Later stories, especially in the Armenian Church, say he preached in Armenia and was martyred there, either beheaded or flayed alive. His relics travelled from the East to Lipari, Benevento and Rome.

## Competing traditions

### Tradition A — "India" (Pantaenus / Eusebius / Jerome)
- **Claim:** Bartholomew evangelised "India" and left a Hebrew Gospel of Matthew there.
- **Earliest source:** Eusebius, *HE* 5.10.3 (c. 320), on Pantaenus' journey of c. 180–190. Eusebius twice hedges ("is said", "it is reported"). **Grade C.** The Catholic Encyclopedia notes: "No mention of St. Bartholomew occurs in ecclesiastical literature before Eusebius."
- **Later:** Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 36 and *Ep.* 70.4 (late 4th c.); Ps-Hippolytus 6; Rufinus 10.9 / Socrates 1.19 ("India" next to Ethiopia).
- **Which India?** Three candidates, none provable:
  1. **South Arabia / Arabia Felix (Yemen)**, a common ancient use of "India". The Catholic Encyclopedia says "India" "was a name covering a very wide area, including even Arabia Felix." Rufinus/Socrates place Bartholomew's India *next to Ethiopia*, which fits the Red Sea region.
  2. **Aksum / Ethiopia.** Socrates 1.19 itself calls Aksum "India interior". The two terms overlapped.
  3. **India proper** (the Malabar coast or the north-west). Jerome's "Brahmans" points to India proper, but Jerome is writing two centuries after the event.
- Pantaenus was reportedly sent "to the nations in the East" (*HE* 5.10.2), and Alexandrian ships traded with all three regions (*Periplus* §§ 4–5, 27, 53–57). So the Pantaenus anecdote is plausible as a trip, whatever its exact destination.

### Tradition B — Armenia
- **Claim:** Bartholomew preached in Greater Armenia, converted King Polymius, and was killed at Albanopolis (Albac, near Başkale) on the orders of the king's brother Astyages.
- **Earliest source traced here:** Ps-Hippolytus 6 ("buried in Allanum, a town of the great Armenia"), date uncertain, late antique. **Grade D.** The Armenian Church's own tradition, which makes him co-founder with Thaddaeus, is **Grade E** in the form traced here. The Armenian historiographical sources for it (e.g. Movses Khorenatsʿi) are UNVERIFIED.
- **Note:** No Arsacid king of Armenia called "Polymius" is known; other versions name Sanatruk. The Greek *Martyrdom* (ANF 8) tells the *same* Polymius story but places it in **India**. The Armenian setting looks like a relocation of one legend.
- **Map warning:** Pleiades "Albanopolis" ([481711](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/481711)) is a town in *Illyria* (Albania), **not** the Armenian Albanopolis. Do not use it. Use Başkale (c. 38.05, 44.02) or Armenia Maior ([863747](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/863747)) instead. Some writers also suggest Caucasian Albania (Azerbaijan), which is UNVERIFIED.

### Tradition C — other regions
Mesopotamia, Persia, Egypt, Lycaonia, Phrygia and the Black Sea are listed by the Catholic Encyclopedia without primary references. **UNVERIFIED. Do not map these without further sources.**

### Bartholomew = Nathanael?
- **For:** In the Synoptic lists Bartholomew is paired with Philip. In John, Philip brings Nathanael (John 1:45). "Bartholomew" is a patronymic ("son of Talmai"), so he could have had another personal name. Nathanael appears among apostles in John 21:2.
- **Against:** John never calls Nathanael an apostle. The identification is not ancient. The Catholic Encyclopedia calls it "ingenious and pretty strong" but notes "the weak link".
- **Grade:** this is a harmonising inference, not a source claim. Present it as "many think…", not as fact.

## Death / martyrdom
Each account is listed separately; none is early.
1. **Beaten with rods and beheaded** in "India" by King Astreges, the brother of Polymius. **D**: *Martyrdom of Bartholomew* (ANF 8).
2. **Crucified head-downwards**, buried at "Allanum… of the great Armenia". **D**: Ps-Hippolytus 6.
3. **Flayed alive (and crucified or beheaded)** at Albanopolis in Armenia by Astyages. **D/E**: Latin *Passio* and Armenian tradition. This is the source of the iconography (e.g. Michelangelo's *Last Judgment* shows him holding his skin). Primary text UNVERIFIED.
4. **Martyred somewhere in "Asia"**, coffin thrown into the sea. **C**: Gregory of Tours, GM 33/34 (no manner of death given).

Unlike for Matthew and Thomas, no early source (such as Heracleon) denies a martyrdom for Bartholomew. But there is no *early* source for his death at all.

**Vreugdenhil, ch. 1:** "Bartholomew, Matthew, Simon the Zealot and Thaddeus all died the martyrs death." He gives no place, no manner and no source. It rests on the later traditions 1–3.

## Open questions / uncertainties
- **Which "India"** did Pantaenus visit? South Arabia, Aksum or India proper? Each is defended by some scholars. Show it on the map as an uncertain area, not as a single point.
- **Armenia** has no early written source in this file. The Armenian sources (Khorenatsʿi etc.) and the Latin *Passio Bartholomaei* still need checking.
- **The Polymius legend** appears in both an Indian and an Armenian setting. Which came first is UNVERIFIED.
- **Nathanael = Bartholomew** is an inference. Find a precise reference for the earliest explicit identification (Isho'dad of Merv, 9th c.?).
- **Relic chain** after Gregory of Tours (Benevento 838, Rome 983/998): secondary sources only.
- **The Martyrdom of Bartholomew's composition date** is unverified.
