# James, the brother of the Lord ("James the Just") — context file

> Context file, not a full apostle timeline. James the brother of the Lord is **not** the
> same person as James son of Zebedee (see `james-zebedee.md`), who was killed c. 44.
> After 44 every "James" in Acts (12:17; 15:13; 21:18) is this James.

Summary (youth group): James was one of Jesus' brothers. During Jesus' ministry his
brothers did not believe in him (John 7:5), but the risen Lord appeared to James
(1 Corinthians 15:7) and he became the leader of the church in Jerusalem. Paul calls him
one of the three "pillars" of the church, together with Peter and John (Galatians 2:9),
and he chaired the meeting in Acts 15 about whether Gentile believers had to be
circumcised. The Jewish historian Josephus tells us he was stoned in Jerusalem in AD 62;
later Christian writers added the story that he was thrown from the temple and prayed
for his killers.

## Competing traditions

### Who were the "brothers of the Lord"?
The NT names James, Joses/Joseph, Simon and Judas as Jesus' brothers (Mark 6:3;
Matt 13:55). Three interpretations exist; we do not pick one.

1. **Sons of Joseph and Mary, born after Jesus** ("Helvidian" view). Basis: the plain
   reading of Mark 6:3; Matt 1:25; Luke 2:7 ("firstborn"). Earliest explicit defender:
   Helvidius (c. 380), known only through Jerome's reply. Tertullian (c. 200) is often
   cited as holding it (*De carne Christi* 7; *Adv. Marcionem* 4.19) — UNVERIFIED wording.
   Common in Protestant scholarship.
2. **Sons of Joseph from an earlier marriage** ("Epiphanian" view). Earliest source:
   *Protevangelium of James* 9.2 (2nd c., grade D): Joseph objects, "I have children,
   and I am an old man". Origen, *Comm. Matt.* 10.17, reports this view from "the Gospel
   according to Peter, or The Book of James". Eusebius *HE* 2.1.2: James "was called the
   brother of the Lord because he was known as a son of Joseph". Standard in Eastern
   Orthodoxy.
3. **Cousins** ("Hieronymian" view). Jerome, *Against Helvidius* (c. 383) and *De vir.
   ill.* 2: James was "the son of Mary sister of the mother of our Lord". Grade C
   (late 4th c., argued exegetically, not from older tradition). Standard in Roman
   Catholic teaching; identifies James with James son of Alphaeus (one of the Twelve).

### Was he one of the Twelve?
- Gal 1:19 ("none of the other apostles except James") can be read as counting James
  as an apostle (in Paul's wider sense) or not. Grade A text, ambiguous meaning.
- Identification with **James son of Alphaeus** (Mark 3:18) depends on the "cousin" view
  above (Jerome). John 7:5 (brothers did not believe) is usually taken as evidence that
  the brother was *not* one of the Twelve during the ministry.
- **Warning for the map:** "James the Less" (Mark 15:40) is a third label that later
  tradition sometimes attaches to this James. Keep him separate from James son of
  Zebedee ("the Greater").

## Death / martyrdom

### Account 1 — Josephus: stoned by order of Ananus II, AD 62
- Source: Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.9.1 (§§197–203), written c. 93–94.
- Content (Whiston tr., as quoted by Eusebius *HE* 2.23.21–24): Festus has died; Nero
  sends Albinus; before he arrives, the high priest Ananus "called together the
  Sanhedrin, and brought before them the brother of Jesus, the so-called Christ, James
  by name, together with some others, and accused them of violating the law, and
  condemned them to be stoned." Moderates complain to King Agrippa II and to Albinus;
  Ananus is deposed after three months.
- Date basis: Festus died in office and Albinus took over in 62 (Albinus procurator
  62–64). This is the standard anchor; the only online confirmation found was Wikipedia
  ("Porcius Festus", procurator c. 59–62) — a printed handbook (e.g. Schürer, *History
  of the Jewish People*) should be cited before publication (UNVERIFIED in print).
- Grade: B* (see grading note).

### Account 2 — Hegesippus (and Clement): thrown from the temple, stoned, clubbed
- Source: Hegesippus, *Memoirs* (*Hypomnemata*) bk 5, c. 170s, preserved in Eusebius,
  *HE* 2.23.4–18. Clement of Alexandria, *Hypotyposes* bk 7 (c. 200), quoted in *HE*
  2.1.4 ("there were two Jameses: one called the Just, who was thrown from the pinnacle
  of the temple and was beaten to death with a club by a fuller, and another who was
  beheaded") and summarised in 2.23.3. Note that Clement already distinguishes the two
  Jameses explicitly.
- Content: scribes and Pharisees set James "upon the pinnacle of the temple" at Passover
  to dissuade the crowd from Jesus; he confesses Jesus as Son of Man at God's right
  hand; they throw him down; "he was not killed by the fall; but he turned and knelt
  down and said, 'I entreat you, Lord God our Father, forgive them, for they know not
  what they do'"; a Rechabite priest cries "Stop… The just one prays for you"; a fuller
  kills him with his club; "they buried him on the spot, by the temple, and his monument
  still remains by the temple… And immediately Vespasian besieged them" (*HE*
  2.23.16–18).
- Grade: B (Hegesippus wrote within ~110 years; legendary features — e.g. James alone
  entering the holy place, *HE* 2.23.6 — are widely regarded as embellishment).
- Date tension: Hegesippus' "immediately Vespasian besieged them" places the death just
  before 67–70, not 62. Most historians follow Josephus for the date.

### Account 3 — "Josephus" saying Jerusalem fell to avenge James
- Origen (*Contra Celsum* 1.47; *Comm. Matt.* 10.17) and Eusebius (*HE* 2.23.20) quote
  Josephus as saying the disasters of 70 happened "to avenge James the Just". **No such
  sentence exists in our manuscripts of Josephus.** Grade C (claim traceable only to
  Origen, c. 230–250). Flag: do not present this as a Josephus quotation.

### Vreugdenhil's version — conflation warning
Vreugdenhil ch. 1 (*The Apostolic Age*): "The Jews killed the apostle James in Jerusalem.
They took him to the roof of the temple and shoved him over the edge… Some writers claim
that James still lived after that fall… flung [stones] at the apostle James… 'Lord, God,
I pray you to forgive them, for they do not know what they do'… one of the Jews… swings
a massive club onto the head of the apostle James."
- This is Hegesippus' account of **James the brother of the Lord** (Eusebius *HE*
  2.23), almost line for line (Rechabite priest: "this righteous man is praying for us").
- Vreugdenhil calls him "the apostle James" and does not distinguish him from James son
  of Zebedee, whose death by Herod's sword (Acts 12:2) he does not narrate in ch. 1.
  Teachers should be told which James the story belongs to.

## Open questions / uncertainties

1. **Vreugdenhil conflation** (see Death): his "apostle James" thrown from the temple is
   the brother of the Lord, not the son of Zebedee. Decide how the map labels this.
2. **Date of death**: 62 (Josephus) vs. just before the siege, c. 66–69 (Hegesippus'
   "immediately Vespasian besieged them"). Majority follows Josephus.
3. **Grade for Josephus**: no slot in the README table (graded B* here).
4. **Pella flight**: historicity debated in modern scholarship (Eusebius' source
   unknown); grade C.
5. **Identity** ("brother" = sibling / step-brother / cousin) is confessionally
   disputed; the map should not decide it.
